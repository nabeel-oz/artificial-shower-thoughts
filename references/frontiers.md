# Frontiers — where a new idea would matter most

This file is the field the `frontier-problem` agent grazes in when a shower runs
in **fresh** mode. It exists because an unconstrained "pick an area" instruction
drifts, in practice, toward whatever is cheap to imagine: small instrumentation
in a narrow professional niche. Read a corpus of such ideas and each one is
defensible; read fifty and the collection has no ambition in it.

The point of this list is not to narrow the shower. It is to make sure the seed
names a **problem that matters to a lot of people**, and lets the drift be as
strange as it likes from there.

The organising frame is Dario Amodei's *Machines of Loving Grace* (2024), which
asks what a genuinely beneficial acceleration would look like, and answers in
five buckets. Those are the buckets below, with the standing bottlenecks in each
— the things that are stuck for structural reasons, not for want of one more
paper. Also worth reading against: the WHO's burden-of-disease priorities, the
Sustainable Development Goals, the *Nature* / Clay-institute style lists of open
problems, and the Millennium/Grand Challenges engineering lists.

## 1. Biology and health

Curing or controlling most of what kills and disables people.

- Diseases with no disease-modifying treatment: most neurodegeneration, most rare monogenic disease, fibrosis, chronic kidney disease.
- Delivery, not discovery: drugs that work but do not reach the patients who need them — cold chain, diagnosis, adherence, cost, distance.
- Antimicrobial resistance, and the broken economics of making new antibiotics.
- The measurement problem in biology: most of what happens inside a living human is inferred from a few blood markers taken at rare intervals.
- Ageing as a modifiable process rather than a backdrop.
- Maternal, neonatal and child mortality where the medicine is known and the system is absent.

## 2. Neuroscience and mental health

- Depression, anxiety, addiction, schizophrenia: mechanisms largely unknown, treatments largely empirical, response rates poor.
- Suffering that is not classified as illness — grief, loneliness, meaninglessness, chronic pain.
- The scale mismatch: therapy works and cannot be manufactured; there are not enough clinicians and there never will be.
- Consciousness, memory, and the brain's own learning rules — genuine gaps in knowledge, not just in application.

## 3. Economic development and poverty

- The gap between what is technically possible and what a poor country can actually run, staff, power and repair.
- Food systems: yield, post-harvest loss, soil, water, protein, and the fragility of supply.
- Energy and its distribution; the same for clean water and sanitation.
- Education that works without a scarce expert in the room.
- Why growth stalls: institutions, capital, corruption, infrastructure, and the reasons known fixes fail on contact with reality.
- Climate adaptation for the people who did least to cause it.

## 4. Peace, governance and institutions

- Democratic institutions under information systems they were not designed for.
- Coordination failures: commons, collective action, the way costs land on people with no say.
- Corruption, capture, and the reasons accountability mechanisms decay.
- Conflict, displacement, and the logistics of a functioning state after one.
- Governing powerful technology without either capture or paralysis.

## 5. Work, meaning and the shape of a life

- What people do, and what it is worth, when capability is cheap.
- Distribution: who holds the gains, and by what mechanism.
- Dignity, agency and belonging as design constraints rather than sentiments.
- Care work — the largest unpaid sector in every economy.
- What education, cities and institutions are for, if the assumptions under them move.

## 6. Knowledge itself

Included because a gap in understanding is as legitimate a seed as a problem in the world.

- Open problems in physics, mathematics and cosmology.
- The reproducibility and incentive failures inside science as a system.
- Fields where the data exists and the theory does not, or the reverse.
- Questions the current toolkit cannot even pose properly.

## Using this file

Pick **one** specific bottleneck, not a bucket. "Neurodegeneration" is a bucket;
"there is no way to know whether a Parkinson's treatment is working until years
of function are already lost" is a bottleneck. The seed should be small enough
to drift from and consequential enough to be worth drifting from.
